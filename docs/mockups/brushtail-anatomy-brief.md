# Mascot anatomy and style

Owner preference: detail between mascot A and C, simplified illustration rather than photorealism. Species must be recognisable as Australian common brushtail possum, Trichosurus vulpecula.

## Sourced features

- Large oval ears with pointed ends; pointed muzzle, pink nose and long whiskers: https://tasland.org.au/programs/wildlife-identification-guide/
- Silver-grey coat, black band across snout, pale to brownish-yellow belly: https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/discovering-wildlife/possum/common-brushtail-possum
- Large pointed ears, dark muzzle and bushy prehensile tail: https://www.australianwildlife.org/animals/common-brushtail-possum
- Wikipedia description also identifies dark muzzle markings and a brown/black tail with a bare underside patch: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_brushtail_possum

Sources vary in describing ears as oval, pointed or tall-rounded; use the actual reference silhouette, not mouse circles or very long rabbit ears. Anatomical terms for prompts: pinnae (external ears), rhinarium (bare nose), vibrissae (whiskers), ventral (underside), prehensile (grasping).

Design interpretation: reduce oversized pink ear interiors and inflated round cheek pouches; simplify the actual tapered muzzle and dark snout markings. Brown eyes remain the owner's preferred treatment. Use expression for charm without giant baby eyes. These proportions are illustration choices, not measurements claimed by the sources.
